International Workshop

AI for Industry

The International Workshop on AI for Industry (AI4I) is a forum for researchers, practitioners, technology providers, and industrial stakeholders working on the design, deployment, evaluation, and governance of AI solutions in real industrial environments.

AI4I welcomes contributions that connect AI research with industrial needs, including scalable AI systems, trustworthy AI, intelligent automation, predictive analytics, digital twins, industrial IoT, human-AI collaboration, and AI-enabled decision support.

Call for Papers

Bridging AI innovation and industrial transformation.

AI4I invites original submissions that explore how artificial intelligence can create value in industrial contexts while addressing practical challenges such as reliability, scalability, safety, trustworthiness, integration, cost, data quality, and organizational adoption.

Scope

AI is reshaping industrial sectors by enabling intelligent automation, predictive maintenance, quality inspection, resource optimization, supply-chain intelligence, autonomous operations, and advanced decision support. However, moving AI from prototypes to production requires more than model accuracy. Industrial AI solutions must operate under real constraints, including legacy systems, limited data, safety requirements, domain-specific regulations, explainability needs, cybersecurity risks, and changing operational environments.

AI4I focuses on research and practice that addresses these challenges. The workshop welcomes contributions on AI methods, architectures, platforms, evaluation approaches, industrial case studies, deployment lessons, and socio-technical perspectives related to the adoption of AI in manufacturing, energy, mobility, healthcare, logistics, construction, agriculture, telecom, smart cities, and other industrial domains.

Submission Types

The workshop welcomes full research papers, short papers, position papers, experience reports, industrial case studies, tool demonstrations, benchmark and dataset papers, and papers describing negative results or lessons learned from real-world deployments.

Submissions should clearly explain the industrial problem, AI approach, evaluation setting, practical relevance, and expected contribution to both research and practice.
